## Short description (Steam allows 300 characters)

Design real pipe and duct networks in every building of a generated city. Outlets that get enough flow earn money, even while you're away. Fix a bad bend and watch the heatmap change. Relaxing, minimal, and built on real engineering math.

## About this game

**Every building in the city needs air, water and gas. You run the pipes.**

Head Loss is a calm, top-down idle game about plumbing and ductwork. Open a building, read what its rooms need, and draw the network: fans and ducts, pumps and risers, regulators and gas lines. Every outlet that receives enough flow pays you, and it keeps paying while you're away.

**The physics is real, and you can see it.** Flow, pressure and friction come from the same equations engineers use. They appear as heatmaps on your pipes and in your rooms. Swap a sharp elbow for a long-radius one and the velocity map shifts in front of you. Nothing is scripted: if a design works on paper, it works in the game.

**Build a city, one network at a time.**

- Start with a hand crank and a tiny house. Grow to family homes, shops, apartment blocks and restaurants.
- Lay water mains, gas mains and power cables across a generated city, and work around its roads and neighbours.
- Design across floors, ceilings and shafts, then pay only when you commit a design.
- Balance overdelivery against your electricity bill. More flow pays more, up to the code limit, and then it pays nothing.

**Trouble is fair, and it's quiet.**

- Run a pipe above its rating and stress builds where you can watch it. Pipes burst because you pushed them.
- Gas leaks fill rooms slowly and visibly. Detectors, automatic shutoffs and good ventilation give you time and options. Ignore an alarm for long enough and the building can explode, taking some of the neighbourhood with it.
- No flashing, no screen shake, no punishing pop-ups. While you're offline, timers pause and wait for you.

**Then start again, better.** Reach a city's milestone to found a new one, with permanent bonuses and new conditions like cold climates and cramped old buildings.
